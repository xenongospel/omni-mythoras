# Mythoras — Imagined Player Statements

> Working design lens, not locked mechanics or terminology. Each statement describes what a system should let the player accomplish before the implementation is designed.

## Scouting

> “I know what role my Squad is missing. I want to narrow where and how to search for a creature that can supply the Skill Card compatibility, utility, frontline resilience, or support my damage dealer needs—without the game simply handing me the exact answer.”

## Creature gear improvement

> “I have found or crafted a promising item for this creature. I want to understand how it advances the role I am building, then invest in turning transitional gear into something strong enough to carry toward endgame.”

## Species-specific advanced identity

> “I have committed to this creature and developed it through the campaign. I want to perform a meaningful, earned transformation that commits its base species to one of its associated advanced identities—not merely select a passive class from a menu.”

The transformation verb and the current **Principal** system name remain unresolved.

## Build commitment

> “I want a major drop to improve this Technician—or inspire me to build another one—not let my current character become anything instantly.”

## World and Workspace views

> “Keep the world clear while I play, but let me surround it with the information I choose when I deliberately want to analyse.”

## Cipher Network

> “Let me choose, modify, and specialise the endgame content I pursue while still discovering what my Squad handles well.”

## Ascension

> “Let me push deeper for exclusive resources that improve my build and expand what I can do elsewhere.”

## Dossier-system targeted progression

> “I know the creature, Skill Card, gearing piece, Agent information, or operating outcome I want. Let me learn which biome or Region to pursue, recognise the stronger sources and whether the whole area or only its boss matters, and make believable progress over an understandable span of play—while leaving enough for me and the community to discover.”
